Abstract
We describe DAI (Decripte Artificial Intelligence), a deployed system in which a software agent conducts
an incident-response episode end to end — curating a signal, extracting a target from structured
evidence, selecting a reviewed playbook, emitting an action contract, having that contract adjudicated by
a decision function that is pure in its declared inputs, executing at a network edge, verifying through a
credential provably incapable of writing, sealing a hash-linked transcript, and returning on a schedule to
review, reconcile, and undo — with no human approving any step.
δ(a,λ,h)
The system's contributions as an artifact are: (i) a decision function whose domain excludes
every learned output, making independence from the model a consequence of the signature rather than
of a runtime check; (ii) separation of duties realized as capability disjointness between two credentials,
verified empirically against the enforcement provider's API before deployment — the verifying credential
receives an authentication error when it attempts to write; (iii) a receipt satisfying a recomputation
contract, recomputed in production by a verifier importing none of the system's code, with exact tamper
localization demonstrated by controlled alteration; (iv) per-principal perimeters inside a shared
enforcement domain, requiring containment predicates that conjoin a hostname discriminator with the
source address, and an aggregation scheme that multiplies simultaneous-containment capacity by the
expression-length limit under a platform rule budget; (v) an element-identified reconciliation
algorithm that remains sound under that aggregation, where the conventional rule-identified algorithm
is unsound in both directions; and (vi) a domain language model trained exclusively on the system's own
outcome-verified transcripts, which narrates and is structurally incapable of deciding.
We report production measurements: a containment executed, verified by a distinct credential, and
sealed 887 ms after the episode's first turn, including two round trips to the enforcement provider; a
receipt of twelve turns recomputed externally with all links valid and, under single-character alteration
of turn zero, failing at exactly the altered index; zero-blast-radius isolation confirmed by the absence of

We also report ten defects found in the period, every one of which was silent, and analyse what each

1. Introduction
1.1 Scope
DAI automates the detection, containment and recovery phases of the standard incident-handling cycle
[3]. It defends infrastructure belonging to its operator and to two sibling products of the same
organization. It ingests signals from network-edge analytics and from a cloud threat-detection service,
opens an episode per material signal, and — where the structured evidence yields an actionable target
and a reviewed playbook covers the signal type — changes the network edge to contain the source.
It is defensive by construction: the catalogue of expressible actions contains only containment and
challenge operations on perimeters its operator controls. There is no reconnaissance, no scanning, no
outward-directed action. This is enforced by a build gate that runs before the test suite, on the reasoning
that if the guarantee that the system does not attack has been weakened, nothing the suite reports
afterwards is relevant.
1.2 DAI is an ADAI, and this is checkable
The companion paper defines ADAI — autonomous defensive artificial intelligence — not as a capability
but as a class with four membership criteria. DAI is an instance of that class. The claim is not rhetorical:
each criterion is checkable, and three of the four are checkable without our cooperation.

1.3 Which problems turned out to be hard
Not the ones anticipated. Deciding what to do is comparatively easy: the space of sensible responses to a
hostile source at a network edge is small, and a reviewed catalogue covers it. The load-bearing problems
were:
1. proving to a party that does not trust the system that what it says it did is what happened (§4);
2. making it structurally impossible for one principal's incident to alter another principal's perimeter, in
a deployment where all three share one enforcement domain (§5);
3. guaranteeing that no effect created in the world is ever orphaned from its record, including when the
process dies between acting and persisting (§6);
4. keeping a learned component in the loop without letting it near the decision (§7).

2.3 Assumed adversary
We adopt the companion paper's threat model without change: the adversary authors signal content,
chooses timing and volume, may hold a compromised operator account, and observes the system's
outputs; the adversary does not hold the executing credential, cannot write below the application to the
evidence store or to the seal, cannot compute second preimages, and does not control the enforcement
provider.
The last exclusion is the system's most significant unmodelled assumption and is worth restating in
concrete terms: verification re-reads the edge through the same provider that executed the change, using
a second credential of that provider. A provider that answered both credentials consistently and falsely
verified
would produce a status for a containment that does not exist, and nothing in the architecture
would detect it. What the construction does exclude is the far more frequent failure — the system being
wrong about what it did — which §8 shows is not hypothetical.

4.3 The receipt
The exported receipt carries: episode metadata; the full chain with all ten hashed fields per turn;
highlighted turns (contract, decision, execution, independent verification); both the stored seal and the
digest recomputed from the chain's tail, separately, so divergence is visible; the algorithm — function,
ordered field list, serialization rules, chaining rule; seven numbered verification steps; and an explicit
statement of limits.
The limits statement is part of the artifact by design. The receipt establishes process: that these turns, in
this order, with these contents, were recorded and not subsequently altered; that the contract preceded
execution; that verification was recorded under a role distinct from the executor. It does not establish
origin: that the signal was true, or that the world matched the description — the distinction between
record integrity and record veracity that the provenance literature makes explicit [21]. It says so, so that
a reader cannot take from it a claim it does not support.

5. Per-principal perimeters in a shared enforcement domain
5.1 The problem, concretely
Multi-tenant isolation is normally studied on the data and storage planes [16]; here the shared resource
is the enforcement point itself.
Three services — the operator's console, a management product, and an offline-capable product — are
served from one DNS zone behind one edge network. All three resolve to the same anycast addresses, so
all three traverse the same enforcement point.
The mechanism available at the outset was domain-level source access rules: predicates of the form
from(x)
, independent of destination. By the discriminator lemma, no such rule is tenant-sound: a rule
raised because of one product's incident denies service to all three, including the console an operator
would use to watch the incident unfold.
5.2 Perimeter bound to principal
Π
The perimeter map is declared in code, not in mutable configuration, and resolved by the episode's
principal with strict equality and no default.
The choice of code over configuration is deliberate and is the same argument used for the system-
consumer identifiers: this map decides where a containment credential may write. In a mutable store,
whoever can write the store can point one principal's perimeter at another's domain — and the
credentials themselves live in that store, so the marginal cost of the stricter placement is zero while the
marginal benefit is that the change passes review and appears in a diff.
There is no "if the principal is unknown, use the operator's perimeter". That default is precisely how one
principal's incident becomes another's outage.

5.6 A correction worth recording
We initially concluded, and reported, that the management product was undefendable at this
enforcement point, on the evidence that a plausibly-named host carried a non-proxied DNS record and
resolved to a single origin address. Both halves were true; the conclusion was wrong, because that host is
not the management product. The product is served at the domain apex, which resolves to the edge
network's anycast addresses.
The error is methodologically interesting and we report it rather than quietly fixing it. It began by
reading a flag on a host whose name was assumed to name the service, and the name was plausible.
Every subsequent check confirmed true facts about the wrong host — including an HTTP request, which
returns an edge-network server header for that name by an unrelated path. What separates the cases is
resolving the name and comparing against the anycast range, and that is now the registration
criterion. The resolution table is recorded alongside the perimeter map so the next reader need not
repeat the measurement.
The correction strengthens §5.3's argument: with the management product at the apex, hostname
scoping is not a convenience but a necessity, since a domain-level rule raised by its incident would cover
the console and the offline product both.

6.3 Hung episodes
An episode in a non-terminal state for more than two hours cannot advance on its own: the message that
opened it has left the queue, and the signal travelled in that message. It is aborted with the reason
written. Two hours is deliberately generous; a genuinely in-flight episode lasts seconds, and aborting one
still working is worse than waiting. This also runs per principal.
Leaving them open is not neutral: the console counts stalled episodes, and a population that never closes
is precisely the noise that teaches an operator to ignore the console — the alarm-fatigue failure
documented in security operations [1] and in clinical monitoring [6].

The distinction between evidence and command is a security property. An interface in which a person's
assertion directly actuates the perimeter is an interface in which compromising that person's account
directly actuates the perimeter.
7. The domain language model
7.1 Constraint
A founder-level constraint requires that no third-party model participate in the cognitive path —
reasoning, decision, action-contract generation, narration. The constraint does not reach voice synthesis
or recognition, which remain external behind a gateway.
Its motivations are recorded and are not ideological. Third-party weights carry third-party acceptable-use
policies, several of which exclude military use, which is disqualifying for the offline product's
government customer. And an external API does not exist on a network with no egress, which is where
that product runs; local inference must therefore be a property of the design rather than a later addition.
7.2 Construction
Tokenizer (byte-pair encoding [29] trained on the system's own corpus), causal Transformer [30], corpus
extraction from sealed transcripts, training with a per-episode validation split so that an episode's turns
never straddle the split, narration with a deterministic fallback, and inference.
Two properties are structural.
DAI | 2026­10­01 | Preprint 15 / 24

Page 16

Outcome-verified corpus. Training reads only episodes with verified outcomes. The learning signal is
not "what the system said" but "what the system did and what a capability-disjoint credential confirmed".
Label quality dominating dataset size is the recurring lesson of applied machine-learning systems work
[28]. This is a much smaller and much more expensive dataset than the alternative, and that is the
point: a corpus of unverified assertions teaches a model to produce assertions.
Approval gate. A trained model is published only after passing a gate on corpus size and diversity. Until
one passes, the narrator falls back to the deterministic rule — and this is the normal state, not an error
state.
7.3 What it may and may not do
It narrates. It cannot decide, by §3.3: the decision function's domain excludes model output. A model
producing a persuasive justification for an inadmissible action changes nothing about admissibility.
Build-time enforcement matches the claim. A gate parses the abstract syntax tree of every module and
refuses imports of third-party model packages — refusing 21 package roots, including pre-trained-weight
loaders, on the reasoning that a third-party dependency need not traverse a network: pre-trained
weights are the same dependency by another path. It also refuses inference-service clients of the cloud
provider, which would be third-party inference without installing anything. It does not refuse the tensor
and numerical libraries: those are the tools the system's own model is built with, and refusing them
would forbid the very thing the constraint mandates.
The gate reads the syntax tree rather than the text because this project's own documents name the
providers it declined to use; a textual search would fail the decision not to use them.
7.4 What we cannot claim
We cannot claim the model beats the deterministic baseline on an adversarial harness, because the
harness is designed and not built, and because the corpus is small. The binding constraint is not
compute: outcome-verified episodes accrue slowly by construction, and the enforcement platform's plan
does not expose the per-address edge events that would most enrich them. This is the corpus-scarcity
open problem of the companion paper, met in practice.
8. Defects found, and what each revealed
Every defect below was silent: the symptom was absence of signal, not presence of error. We report them
because in a system of this kind they are the most informative part, and because the argument for the
architecture is precisely that it converts silent failures into loud ones.
8.1 Perimeter not bound to principal
The edge adapter was constructed once from process configuration and used for every episode, without
consulting the episode's principal. Read isolation was complete and tested; no principal had ever read
another's data. The action path had no equivalent check.
Latent while one principal produced signal; reachable the moment API keys were issued to two sibling
products — which is what the keys existed for.
DAI | 2026­10­01 | Preprint 16 / 24

Page 17

What it revealed. Isolation had been audited entirely on the path where auditing is cheap, because every
query carries a visible filter, and assumed on the path that changes the world. Auditing the queries
would have returned green. The class is general: a guarantee verified where verification is easy and
assumed where it is expensive.
8.2 A receipt no one could recompute
The receipt reused the console's display projection, which carries five of the ten hashed fields.
Recomputation per the receipt's own instructions produced five failures out of five turns.
Every internal test passed, because every internal test constructed the turn shape by hand or through the
system's own serialization.
What it revealed. A property of an artifact, tested through its producer, is untested. The remedy has two
parts: the test now calls the real projection rather than reconstructing its shape, and conformance is
demonstrated by a verifier importing none of the system's code.
8.3 A query for a value the writer never writes
The review query selected turns whose kind literal differed by a prefix from the one the writer persists.
SQL does not object to a literal matching nothing, so the lateral join returned the empty set, silently, for
weeks.
Two consequences. Executed containments were never reviewed — the loop closed only through the
adoption branch, which is why the testimony cycle of §6.4 worked and gave false confidence. And the
guard against re-adoption used the same literal and was therefore permanently false, so the same effect
was re-adopted every hour: production logs show one rule adopted at 20:44, 20:56, 21:56 and 22:55,
with "reconciled: 0" each time.
The same class of error existed in two further places: the connector screen's "last containment" read a
kind that never existed and was permanently null, and the receipt's execution highlight was never
populated (the full chain was always present, so the proof was unaffected; the index pointed at nothing).
No test caught any of it, because all used a storage double, and a double returns what the test specifies
— the classic hazard of mock-based testing [19]. The thing that was wrong was the literal, visible only
against the real store or against the source text.
Remedy. All three now derive the value from a single enumeration, and the review passes it as a bound
parameter. A test reads the source and fails any kind literal the writer cannot produce.
What it revealed. A defect in the interface between two components, each individually correct and
individually tested. Doubles encode the implementer's belief about the other side; where that belief is the
defect, doubles are blindfolds.
8.4 A documented guarantee that was never enforced
A design record asserted that a build gate covered the model layer and refused third-party model SDKs.
The gate checked something else entirely. The absence of such SDKs was in fact true — but by discipline,
not by enforcement.
Remedy. The gate described in §7.3 now exists.
DAI | 2026­10­01 | Preprint 17 / 24

Page 18

What it revealed. Documentation that switches off inspection. A reader who believes "the gate covers
this" stops checking it, and the cost appears not when the statement becomes false but when someone
relies on it. The corresponding process rule — every asserted guarantee carries an enforcing check, or
states in its own text that it is discipline and not a gate — is now recorded as a decision rather than left
as a habit.
8.5 Silent loss of the adversary's address
Two access paths to the same provider's threat findings use different field capitalizations. The reader
used one; the delivery path produced the other. Every finding lost the source address silently — and the
test passed, because its double mixed both spellings, a shape the provider never emits.
Remedy. Fixtures captured verbatim from the account.
What it revealed. A double that encodes the implementer's belief about an external format, rather than
the format.
8.6 Scheduled components running stale code
The deployment pipeline updated two long-running services and no scheduled tasks; each task definition
remained pinned to whatever artifact a human last registered. The task closing the containment loop and
the task reading the threat feed both lagged the tested tree — so §8.5's fix had been merged, tested,
deployed to both services, and never reached the component that used it.
Remedy. One deployment mechanism covers every component that runs, and reports what it changed.
Registering a new task revision suffices, since the schedule targets the family rather than a pinned
revision.
What it revealed. Divergence between the tested artifact and the running artifact, along a path no test
observes.
8.7 A lost update with no symptom
Containment under aggregation is read-modify-write over a set with no compare-and-swap available, so
two overlapping episodes on the same principal can lose one containment; the write reports success, and
only independent verification notices. §5.7 gives the mechanism and the mitigation.
What it revealed. Two things. First, that a decision taken for one reason — aggregating addresses into
one rule to fit a rule budget — carries consequences in an unrelated dimension, and that the second
consequence is easy to miss precisely because the first one is the interesting one. Second, that "there is
only one writer" was true and was not a property of this component: it was a process count. A guarantee
that holds because of how something is deployed is a guarantee that disappears in a scaling decision
taken by someone who never read this code.
How it was found. By writing the formal account of aggregation for the companion paper and noticing
that the same premise yields a second theorem. It had no symptom and would have had none until the
first overlap.

Page 19

8.8 The receipt does not authenticate whose session it is
The digest covers ten fields of each turn. The principal is not one of them: the turn carries the session
identifier, and the session record carries the principal. A third party recomputing a receipt per §4.4
therefore confirms what happened and cannot confirm on whose behalf.
The binding is real but local — an append-only constraint on the store — and a local guarantee is worth
nothing to an external reader, who has no access to the store and no way to observe that the constraint
exists. In a system whose strongest claim is per-principal isolation (§5), that is the wrong thing to leave
outside the authenticated set.
What it revealed. That "the receipt proves X" and "the system enforces X" are different sentences, and that
the second was quietly being read as the first — by us. The append-only constraint had been treated as if
it made attribution part of the evidence, and it does not; it makes attribution part of the operator's trust
in their own database.
Status: not fixed. Adding the principal to the hashed field set requires recomputing every historical
digest, which is precisely what an append-only store exists to prevent — it is a fork of the chain, not an
edit. Binding the session record into turn zero's payload fixes it forward, leaves history alone, and is
what we intend to do. We report it unfixed rather than describe the intention as the state.
8.9 Target validation narrower than the predicate it discharged
Target validation rejected addresses that are private, loopback, link-local, multicast, reserved or
unspecified — the addresses that are not routable sources — and was taken to discharge the bounded-
effect-scope predicate. It admitted addresses inside the enforcement provider's own published ranges.
Two containments against the provider's shared proxy addresses stood in the zone until found by
inspection.
Nothing in the reasoning path erred, which is what makes this defect worth the space. The evidence was
structured; extraction was sound; the proposed target was the address the evidence named, and under a
proxied path the observed source is the proxy. The action was still outside the predicate: a shared proxy
address has blast radius far above one, since the effect falls on every principal arriving through it —
including the defended principal — and it is additionally the path by which the containment would be
verified, so the effect and its verification become mutually dependent.
What it revealed. That a check can exist, be correct about what it checks, and be a proper subset of the
predicate it is credited with enforcing. §8.4 was documentation asserting a check that did not exist; this
is the mirror image, and the harder one to see: the implemented subset is exactly the subset a reviewer
thinks to look for. It is also why the envelope-integrity gate attacks the predicates rather than reading the
code that implements them — the defect had been readable in the source for as long as the source
existed.
Status: fixed. The provider's published ranges are enumerated as static, dated data on the decision path,
with a tool that refreshes them by reviewed diff. Static and not resolved at decision time: a live lookup
δ
would make depend on a network service and cease to be pure in its declared inputs, which would
break both §3.3 and the recomputation contract. The case is now in the envelope harness, where its
absence had been invisible.

The two standing containments are a separate matter from the defect: removing them is an action, and it
goes through review and undo like any other, not through an operator editing the provider's
configuration behind the system's back.
8.10 A guarantee that holds by construction and cannot be checked from the receipt
Evidence-Bound Targeting holds. The extraction function's domain is a fixed key set over structured
evidence; free-text fields are not in it, and a constructed attack naming an address only in an adversary-
influenced field is refused.
What does not hold is third-party verification of it. The transcript's curation turn records the signal's
summary, severity, type and source reference — not the raw_content the parser read. Checked on an
exported production receipt: the contained address appears in the hypothesis summary, in the action
contract and in narration, and nowhere in any evidence field, because no turn carries one.
The consequence is exact. A recipient of a receipt recomputes the chain, confirms that a contract
preceded a decision which preceded an effect which was verified by a disjoint credential — and then
must take the emitter's word for where the target came from. That is the one thing the receipt exists to
make unnecessary.
What it revealed. That the guarantee holds and the guarantee is provable to a stranger are different claims,
and a system can satisfy the first while the second quietly does not apply. The distinction is invisible until
something evaluates the receipt against the mechanisms rather than only against itself: the chain verifies
perfectly either way, because the missing field was never hashed — it was never there.
Status: not fixed. The repair is to record the structured evidence, or a digest of it, in the curation turn —
bringing it inside the hashed field set so the target's provenance becomes recomputable. This is a change
to what is hashed, and therefore to the recomputation contract, which is why it is reported rather than
applied: the contract is versioned, and historical receipts must keep verifying under the version they
were sealed with.
Until then, EBT is reported as holding by construction and not verifiable from the record — which is what
it is, and is more useful than either of the two comfortable simplifications.

10. What is not built
An inventory of capability written by its builders is worth nothing without its complement.
Adversarial harness with blind holdout. Designed, not built. Until it exists, no claim about policy
quality is supported, and §7.4 stands.
Reconciliation against a world of foreign granularity. The hostname case is solved (§6.2); the
general problem is not.
Per-address edge events. Unavailable on the enforcement platform's current plan, and the binding
constraint on corpus diversity.
Versioned infrastructure. Several scheduled task definitions and one execution role exist in the
account without a versioned declaration; the provisioning script lags the tree.
A third perimeter. Exhausted by rule budget, not by design (§5.6).
This inventory perishes quickly. Between the first draft of these notes and their first revision, on the same
day, the system executed its first real containment and a sentence stating that none had occurred
stopped being true.
11. Conclusion
DAI acts on production infrastructure without a human approving each action, and the argument that
this is acceptable is not that its model is good. It is that the decision is made outside the model by a pure
function of a declared contract; that the credential which verifies is provably incapable of executing; that
the perimeter it may write to is bound to the principal it acts for, with no default; that per-principal
containment is expressed by conjoining a discriminator with the source, so blast radius is one by
construction; that every effect is reachable from a hash-linked record a third party can recompute
without the system's code; and that every effect is revisited, reconciled, and reversible.
The defects of §8 are the best available evidence for that structure, because every one was silent and
every one was silent for the same reason: the guarantee lived in convention rather than in construction.
The system's claim on attention is not that it does not fail. It is that it fails loudly — fail-stop rather than
fail-silent [26, 9].
Limitations
Production measurements come from a single deployment over days; they are instances, not statistics.
The claims of §3.3 and §5.4 are architectural and hold for the system as constructed. The external
recomputation of §4.4 was performed for one receipt and one controlled alteration; it demonstrates the

property, not its frequency. No claim is made about policy quality, which is unmeasured (§7.4), and none
about sufficiency for safety — only that each named component is necessary for the guarantee it names.
